Module 3

Understand Your Users Before Building the Solution

03 / 07

Introduction

Now that you've identified the problem, you may feel excited to start designing a solution.

But first, ask yourself: Who am I designing this solution for?

A problem may seem clear from your perspective, but the people experiencing it may see it differently. That's why successful innovators take time to understand their users before designing any solution.

What Will You Learn?

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

  • Identify the real users of your project.
  • Understand the problem from their perspective.
  • Gather information that helps you understand their needs.
  • Write a clear problem statement before designing a solution.

Step 1: Identify Your Users

Your users are the people or organizations experiencing the problem and looking for a solution.

They may be students, teachers, doctors, patients, companies, or even entire communities.

The more clearly you define your users, the easier it becomes to design an effective solution.

Example

Instead of saying: "I want to develop an educational app." Ask yourself: who is it for?

Is it for primary school students, high school students, university students, or teachers?

Each group has different needs, so one solution cannot meet everyone's needs.

Step 2: Understand the Problem from Your Users' Perspective

Don't rely on assumptions. Instead, try to understand how your users experience the problem in their daily lives.

Ask yourself:

  • How do they deal with the problem today?
  • What frustrates them the most?
  • What solutions are they currently using?
  • What is missing from those solutions?

The better you understand the problem, the more likely you are to design a successful solution.

Step 3: Listen More Than You Speak

If you have the opportunity to talk with your users, focus on listening before presenting your idea.

You can ask questions such as:

  • How do you deal with this problem?
  • What is the most difficult part?
  • What would you like to change?
  • Have you tried other solutions?

Their answers may completely change the way you think about the problem.

Step 4: Don't Rely Only on Your Opinion

Innovators don't make decisions based on assumptions.

Instead, they rely on:

  • Observation.
  • User feedback.
  • Research and evidence.
  • Testing.

This helps ensure the solution is built around real needs rather than personal assumptions.

Step 5: Write a Clear Problem Statement

Before starting your research, write a brief description of the problem.

Try to answer these questions:

  • What is the problem?
  • Who is affected?
  • Where does it occur?
  • Why is it worth solving?

Example

Instead of writing: "There is a problem in schools."

Write: "Some high school students struggle to manage their time between schoolwork and extracurricular activities, affecting their ability to complete tasks and prepare for exams."

The clearer your problem statement is, the easier it will be to develop an effective solution.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming you already understand users' needs without asking them.
  • Designing a solution for too broad an audience.
  • Relying on personal opinions instead of research and observation.
  • Building a solution before fully understanding the problem.

Module Summary

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Understand why knowing your users is essential to innovation.
  • Recognize that successful solutions begin with understanding users' needs.
  • Use listening and observation to better understand the real problem.
  • Write a clear problem statement that guides the next stages of your project.

Next Step

You now have a clear understanding of both the problem and the people affected by it.
