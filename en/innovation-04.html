Prerequisite

Module 3 – Understand Your Users Before Building the Solution.

Introduction

Now that you've identified the problem and understood your users, don't move straight to designing a solution.

Start with research.

Someone may have already studied the problem or developed solutions you can learn from. Scientific research helps you build an innovation based on knowledge rather than assumptions.

What Will You Learn?

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

  • Understand the importance of scientific research in innovation.
  • Write a clear research question.
  • Choose reliable sources.
  • Identify a research gap.
  • Organize information to support the development of your project.

Step 1: Start with a Clear Question

Before you begin your research, ask yourself: What do I want to learn?

The more specific your question is, the more useful your results will be.

Example

Instead of searching for "Education", ask: "Why do some students struggle to manage their study time?"

Step 2: Choose Reliable Sources

Use reliable sources, such as:

  • Scientific research papers.
  • University websites.
  • Government agencies.
  • International organizations.
  • Recent academic books.

Avoid relying on unverified information or social media as your primary source.

Step 3: Learn from Existing Solutions

Review existing solutions and ask yourself:

  • What works well?
  • What could be improved?
  • Is there a problem that still hasn't been solved?

Studying previous solutions helps you develop an idea that creates real value.

Step 4: Identify a Research Gap

As you review different sources, look for questions or challenges that have not been fully addressed.

A research gap may become an opportunity for a new innovation.

Step 5: Organize Your Research

Organize the information you collect, including:

  • The source.
  • Publication date.
  • Key findings.
  • Your notes and questions.

Keeping your research organized from the beginning will make it easier to develop your project and write your research paper later.

Common Mistakes

  • Relying on only one source.
  • Using outdated or unreliable information.
  • Copying information without citing the original source.
  • Ignoring evidence that challenges your idea.

Module Summary

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Understand why scientific research is the foundation of successful innovation.
  • Write a clear research question that leads to better results.
  • Use reliable sources to build a stronger project.
  • Learn from existing solutions to develop better ideas.
  • Organize your research for the next stages of your project.

Next Step

You now understand the problem, your users, and what previous research has discovered.

In the next module, you'll learn how to generate creative ideas, compare different solutions, and choose the one that best addresses your problem.
